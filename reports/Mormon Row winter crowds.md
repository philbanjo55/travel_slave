# A one-mile walk empties Mormon Row

In late December the road closure does the crowd control at Mormon Row. Cars are stopped about a mile before the barns, so the summer and autumn scene (20 or more photographers and a full lot at first light) mostly goes away. Expect to share a holiday-week sunrise at the T.A. Moulton or John Moulton barn with **anywhere from no one to a handful of independent photographers who walked or snowshoed in**. In the daytime there will be a scattering of walkers, skiers and snowshoers. No source counts winter visitors at the barns, so that range is an inference. It rests on three solid facts. First, the National Park Service closes the district to vehicles "once snow arrives" until spring, about 1 mile before the Mormon Row intersection ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/morowcl.htm)). Second, December is one of the park's quietest months, at roughly 49,000-50,000 visits, about 7% of July ([NationalSitesGuide](https://www.nationalsitesguide.com/sites/grand-teton/best-time-to-visit/)). Third, every dated winter photo workshop found runs in mid-November to early December, not Christmas to New Year. The one real crowd risk is timing: 29-31 Dec falls in Jackson's busiest winter week, so a clear post-storm morning then is the likeliest time to find tracks and tripods ahead of you. On 2-4 Jan it should be quieter. One caveat runs through all of this. This session could read nps.gov but not most other sites (irma.nps.gov, the Jackson Hole News&Guide, Buckrail, TripAdvisor, forums, operator websites). Much of the evidence below therefore comes from search-engine summaries, not full pages, and is marked as such.

## Cars stop a mile out, and that does the crowd control

The NPS states the access rule plainly. From the first snow until the spring melt, the Mormon Row Historic District is closed to vehicles about 1 mile before the Mormon Row intersection. Visitors use a **"parking pullout about 1 mile west on Antelope Flats Road"**, and both ceremony sites are "inaccessible by vehicle in the winter" ([NPS Mormon Row Ceremony Location](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/morowcl.htm)). The park's road page lists Antelope Flats among roads "closed seasonally based on conditions", with no dates. It also confirms that US 89/191 is plowed from Jackson to Flagg Ranch, so the drive from Jackson to the Antelope Flats turn stays open all winter ([NPS Road Information](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/roads.htm)). The park's winter page names Antelope Flats Road as a place to cross-country ski and snowshoe ([NPS Winter in Grand Teton](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/winter.htm)).

Sources give slightly different distances on foot. A local guide says the road is **"only plowed for the first mile to a parking turnout"**, and you ski or snowshoe on from there ([JacksonHoleWY.net](https://www.jacksonholewy.net/park_highlights/mormon_row.php)). The Teton Photography Club calls it "only 3/4 mile to the Mormon Row intersection from the ample parking area" ([Teton Photography Club](https://tetonphotographyclub.org/mormon-row-in-winter/)). A TripAdvisor forum poster puts it at about a mile past the plow turnaround, depending on snow depth ([TripAdvisor forum](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g143029-i1019-k14973887-Hike_to_Mormon_Row_in_Winter-Grand_Teton_National_Park_Wyoming.html)). Those three were seen as search summaries only. Plan on **roughly 0.75-1 mile to the junction, plus a few hundred metres to each barn, so a 2-3 mile round trip on snow**. No official source gives the exact plow end point, the size of the pullout, or whether the closure is a gate or just the end of plowing. The forum thread also mentions a longer southern way in, from Gros Ventre Road toward Kelly. It is unconfirmed, and it may brush against the Kelly Hill wildlife closure.

The notes disagree about which barn is north. The NPS ceremony page describes the North site as reached "a short walk down the gated road past the 'Pink House'", and the Pink House stands at the John Moulton homestead ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/morowcl.htm)). That fits the reading that **John Moulton (with the pink house) lies north of the Antelope Flats junction and T.A. Moulton south of it**. One guide adds that the T.A. Moulton barn is easy to reach "except in the winter" ([Photographer's Trail Notes](https://photographerstrailnotes.com/jackson-hole-yellowstone/ta-moulton-barn), search summary). Check this against the trip's own map pins before the walk in.

Wildlife closures do not block the route. The current closure list, updated 9 Dec 2025, shuts the Snake River, Buffalo Fork and Kelly Hill areas to all entry from **15 Dec to 31 Mar**. Mormon Row, Antelope Flats and Blacktail Butte are not on it ([NPS Temporary & Wildlife Closures](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/wildlife-closures.htm)). The closure therefore matters only for anyone trying the Kelly-side approach.

## December is quiet, but the holiday week is Jackson's peak

The park-wide numbers set the baseline. Secondary sites built on NPS statistics put December at **about 49,300 recreation visits (2021-2025 average), roughly 7% of July's ~732,000** ([NationalSitesGuide](https://www.nationalsitesguide.com/sites/grand-teton/best-time-to-visit/)). Another puts it at 50,100 (2019-2024), with February higher at 54,200 ([TravelBinger](https://travelbinger.com/ideal-times-visit-national-parks)). Both were seen as summaries. The official counts on irma.nps.gov could not be opened. Roughly 50,000 visits works out to about 1,600 a day across the whole park. The traffic counters also count people driving US-191 through the park, so only a small share of those visitors will walk a mile across Antelope Flats.

Cold weather cuts the numbers further. The NPS put December 2016 visitation **down 18% on December 2015, "likely explained by extreme cold temperatures which deterred visitors"**. Some traffic counters failed in that same frigid spell ([NPS 2016 release](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/news/2016-visitation-sets-record.htm)). In a hard cold snap over the trip week, expect fewer people at the barns, not more.

The holiday is the factor that pushes the other way. The Jackson Hole News&Guide calls the week between Christmas and New Year's **"traditionally one of the busiest times of the season"** ([JH News&Guide](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/business/lodging-bookings-begin-to-rise-as-snow-flies/article_1f90e2d4-bf56-11ef-b8cd-a3f6ccf4b0e8.html), summary, year unconfirmed). A Buckrail report on winter 2020-21 put New Year's weekend lodging at **76.1% occupancy**, and said "the first week of January we see a drop off" ([Buckrail](https://buckrail.com/jackson-hole-tourism-reports-for-dec-projections-for-winter-2021/), summary). Most of these visitors come to ski at the resort, not to snowshoe to a barn. Even so, the pattern points to **29-31 Dec as the busiest mornings of the trip and 2-4 Jan as the quietest**. No source gives holiday-week traffic counts inside the park, and the NPS has published nothing on Mormon Row winter visitation.

## Witness accounts describe solitude, but none count heads in winter

The crowd figures that exist all describe the seasons when the road is open. One photographer's guide expects **"between 5 to 20 other photographers at sunrise"** at the T.A. Moulton barn "depending on the season" ([Photographer's Trail Notes](https://photographerstrailnotes.com/jackson-hole-yellowstone/ta-moulton-barn)). A TripAdvisor review counted "probably 20 people when they arrived, with more coming closer to sunrise" ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowUserReviews-g60838-d2329299-r230612026-Mormon_Row_Historic_District-Moose_Grand_Teton_National_Park_Wyoming.html)). An October 2025 visitor found "so many people there that it wasn't worth it" ([TripAdvisor reviews](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g60838-d2329299-Reviews-Mormon_Row_Historic_District-Moose_Grand_Teton_National_Park_Wyoming.html)). Another guide warns that at first light "the parking lot and the road is packed with many cars" ([The Mountains Please](https://themountainsplease.com/mormon-row-sunrise-photography/)). The NPS itself says parking "can be very limited especially at sunrise, sunset and midday" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/morowcl.htm)). In winter those lots sit behind the closure.

The winter accounts all point the other way, though none gives a number. The Teton Photography Club writes that "by early winter, most of the visitors are back in their cozy homes". Its search summary adds that a winter hiker will likely be the only person around, a line that could not be read on the page itself ([Teton Photography Club](https://tetonphotographyclub.org/mormon-row-in-winter/)). A December 2007 gallery describes a Moulton barn sunrise reached by a hike in **"-26 degrees through modest snow"** and mentions no one else ([Ed Fuhr](https://www.edfuhr.com/gallery/moultonbarn/)). A January 2014 reviewer walked "in very deep snow" around the barns at 5 am and again for sunset, also mentioning no one else ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowUserReviews-g60838-d2329299-r191954415-Mormon_Row_Historic_District-Moose_Grand_Teton_National_Park_Wyoming.html)). One Locationscout listing rates the winter crowd as "just a few people" ([Locationscout](https://www.locationscout.net/usa/245-mormon-row-barns), summary). A Grand Teton photographer calls the area "even more serene this time of year" ([Lisa Blount Photography](https://www.lisablountphotography.com/blogs/travel/mormon-row-ta-moulton-barn-travel), summary). None of these covers Christmas to New Year week. Reddit, FredMiranda, Flickr and YouTube returned nothing usable or could not be reached.

Daytime traffic is just as uncounted. The likeliest picture is walkers on the closed-road stretch plus a few skiers and snowshoers. Footprints are the real unknown for a photographer. A direct search for footprints or tracks around the barns in winter found only stock galleries ([Jackson Hole Photography Gallery](https://www.jacksonholephotographygallery.com/mormon-row-barns)). The January 2014 "very deep snow" report hints that the snow can stay unbroken. The inference is that tracks gather along the road and on the classic straight-on east-side positions, while snow off the usual axes stays clean after a fresh fall. That is reasoning only. One general snow-photography tip applies: "act fast when snow is fresh" ([Adorama](https://www.adorama.com/alc/tips-snow-photography)).

## Tours drive past, and workshops avoid the holiday week

Commercial traffic in late December is vehicle-based, and it stops at the plowing. Jackson Hole Wildlife Safaris caps winter tours at **8 guests per vehicle**, and its photography experience at 6 ([JHWS](https://jacksonholewildlifesafaris.com/winter-safaris/winter-wildlife-safari/), summary). Private winter half-day tours sold through resellers name Mormon Row as a stop ([Viator](https://avionero.partner.viator.com/tours/Jackson/Private-Winter-Half-Day-Wildlife-Safari-From-Jackson-Hole/d51006-13316P6); [Travelocity](https://www.travelocity.com/things-to-do/grand-teton-private-winter-tour.a49261994.activity-details); [Adventure Collective](https://app.advguides.com/marketplace/grand-teton-private-winter-wildlife-tour-snake-river-elk-flats-mormon-row)). Teton Science Schools runs a winter sunrise wildlife tour; one page gives a **7:30 AM MST (9:30 AM EST)** start from Jackson, though no listing says it stops at Mormon Row ([TSS](https://www.tetonscience.org/tours/winter-grand-teton-sunrise-wildlife-tour/), summary). One BrushBuck dawn listing caps its group at 12 ([BrushBuck](https://brushbucktours.rezdy.com/catalog/168845/winter-multi-day), summary). These vans would reach Antelope Flats around 8 AM MST, after sunrise, and at most view the barns from the turnout. No guided ski or snowshoe trip to the barns turned up.

The photo workshops avoid the holiday week entirely. Joseph Marc's early-winter workshop (16-20 Nov 2026, groups of 1-5) plans a T.A. Moulton sunrise "when weather allows" ([Joseph Marc](https://josephmarcphotographyllc.wetravel.com/i/68bddb23d249385553b452b6)). Matt Suess ran 1-6 Dec 2024 and 30 Nov - 5 Dec 2025 ([WeTravel](https://www.wetravel.com/trips/2024-grand-teton-winter-wildlife-landscape-photography-workshop-the-foto-experience-with-matt-suess-92431063)). The NANPA-listed Vanuga/Gocke tour ran 4-7 Dec 2025 and included the Moulton barn ([NANPA](https://nanpa.org/eventcalendar/grand-teton-np-in-winter-photography-tour-december-4-7-2025/)). Backcountry Journeys runs in November ([Backcountry Journeys](https://backcountryjourneys.com/destinations/Rocky-Mountains/Bull-Moose-Workshop-Winter-Wildlife-of-Grand-Teton)). **Every dated winter workshop found falls between mid-November and about 7 December.**

The park's own rules suggest why. Grand Teton's workshop operating plan limits groups to **10 participants plus a leader**. It allows walks of **"½ mile or less"** on official trails from designated turnouts, and it says visitors "may not be asked to move" ([NPS 2024 Operating Plan](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/upload/2024-Operating-Plan-Photography-Painting-Workshops.pdf)). The barns sit roughly 0.75-1 mile past the winter turnout. The likely reading is that a licensed workshop cannot walk clients out to them once the road closes, so workshops shoot the barns while it is still open. No source states this. The NPS Business Resources Branch (307-739-3417) can confirm it. Either way, **expect no organised groups at the barns at a holiday-week sunrise**.

## Construction is paused, but its fences may still be in the frame

A paved parking lot, restrooms, shade structures, sidewalks, picnic tables and fencing are going in at Mormon Row. The NPS lists the district as "Open, with some access to buildings closed. Construction ongoing", with a June 2026 - 2027 timeframe ([NPS Park-Wide Construction](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/park-construction.htm), summary; [GTNP Foundation](https://www.gtnpf.org/blog-post/mormon-row-summer2026); [Cowboy State Daily](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/05/06/toilets-parking-and-roads-grand-teton-starts-multi-year-improvement-projects/)). The park's conditions page, updated 23 Nov 2026, says **construction is paused from 15 Nov 2026 to 1 May 2027**, though some areas "will remain closed due to site conditions and safety concerns" ([NPS Current Conditions](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)). No crews should be there over the trip week. Half-built fencing, staging areas or closed buildings may still stand near the barns, and no source shows exactly where. Snow may hide some of it, but nothing confirms that. Check the conditions page in the last week of December, and look for new fence lines in the first frame of the morning.

## A practical dawn plan for a black-and-white 4x5 shooter

The plan below is worked out from the sources; the light times are computed, not taken from a web page. At the barns (43.665 N, 110.666 W) on 29 Dec - 4 Jan, flat-horizon times are as follows. The Tetons lie due west, so the sun rises behind the camera and lights the peaks head-on. First light touches the summits a few minutes before flat-horizon sunrise, and the sun stays low all day (about 23° at noon), so low side light on the barns' east faces lasts well into the morning.

| Moment | Mountain (MST) | Eastern (EST) |
|---|---|---|
| Park at the Antelope Flats turnout | ~6:30 AM | ~8:30 AM |
| Leave on snowshoes, headlamp on | ~6:45 AM | ~8:45 AM |
| Nautical dawn | 6:47-6:48 AM | 8:47-8:48 AM |
| At the barn, set up and focus | ~7:15 AM | ~9:15 AM |
| Civil dawn | 7:24 AM | 9:24 AM |
| Alpenglow on the Grand | ~7:45-7:57 AM | ~9:45-9:57 AM |
| Sunrise (bearing 121-122°) | 7:56-7:57 AM | 9:56-9:57 AM |
| Wildlife vans may reach Antelope Flats | ~8:00 AM onward | ~10:00 AM onward |

Snowshoes suit a heavy 4x5 pack better than skis. They also make it easier to step off the road into deep snow for a foreground. The NPS asks snowshoers to stay off ski tracks and travel beside them, warns that "winter trails are neither marked nor flagged", and says to keep 25 yards from wildlife and 100 yards from wolves and bears ([NPS skiing & snowshoeing](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/skiing-and-snowshoeing.htm)). The flat is exposed, and its "wide-open plain can kick with wind" ([Moon](https://www.moon.com/?p=578666), summary). The NPS warns that hypothermia and frostbite "can set in quickly" and that going alone is especially risky ([NPS Winter](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/winter.htm)). Leave the plan with someone, and note that 911 works in the park.

Protect the foreground. Walk in along the road, then reach the camera position along the fence or road edge, so the snow between lens and barn stays untouched. After a fresh snowfall, the first person in decides the foreground for everyone, so a clear morning after a storm is the one worth the earliest start. Off-axis positions, like Phil's saved north-east T.A. Moulton spot, are less likely to be trampled than the straight-on east view. If someone is already set up, don't step in front of them. The NPS "visitors may not be asked to move" rule binds workshops, and it is good manners for everyone. The park also bans attaching gear to historic buildings and light painting for workshops, and the same restraint is wise for independents ([NPS Operating Plan](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/upload/2024-Operating-Plan-Photography-Painting-Workshops.pdf)).

For the film itself, large-format users report several cold-weather problems ([Photrio](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/large-format-at-low-temperature.4839/); [LargeFormatPhotography.info](https://www.largeformatphotography.info/forum/archive/index.php/t-44077.html); [Photrio sub-zero thread](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/shooting-in-sub-zero-conditions.35834)):

- **Brittle film:** cold film cracks; the emulsion itself is fine. Load holders indoors and pull dark slides slowly.
- **Static marks:** dry cold air causes them, so handle film slowly.
- **Frosted ground glass:** breath condenses and freezes on it at once. Use a focusing cloth and loupe, and hold your breath.
- **Slow shutters:** they drag below about -20 °C (-4 °F). An old shutter is worth a cold-weather service.
- **Dead meter batteries:** keep the meter warm between readings.
- **Condensation indoors:** the biggest risk comes after the shoot, when cold gear goes back inside. Bag the camera and holders before entering the car or room.

For black and white, the front-lit peaks against a still-dark western sky separate strongly. A red or orange filter deepens the sky after sunrise. Placing the snow at Zone VII-VIII keeps texture. Expect reciprocity correction in the blue hour. Valley inversion fog is the main sky risk. The project's own Jackson airport history logs fog in about 8-9% of 08-09 MST (10-11 EST) hours in December and January, and that is the one condition that empties the morning of both people and peaks.

## Conclusion

The crowd question at Mormon Row in holiday week comes down to how far people are willing to walk, not how many are in the park. The mile on foot removes the parking-lot and tripod-line crowds of summer. The ½-mile workshop rule appears to remove organised groups too, which explains the gap between workshops that end in early December and an empty holiday calendar. What remains is a small, self-selected group of photographers who will snowshoe in the dark. They are most likely on a clear morning after fresh snow between 29 and 31 Dec, exactly the morning Phil would choose anyway. The practical contest is therefore not for elbow room but for untracked snow, and being first on the flat wins it.

Several evidence gaps remain, and they can be closed before the trip. Allowing irma.nps.gov would give exact December counts by year. Allowing tripadvisor.com, reddit.com and flickr.com would allow dated winter photos to be checked for tracks and head counts. A look at the NPS conditions page and a call to the park (307-739-3399) in late December would confirm how far the road is plowed and where the construction fencing stands. Until then, the head-count ranges in this report are inference from sourced access rules and seasonal patterns, not observation.
