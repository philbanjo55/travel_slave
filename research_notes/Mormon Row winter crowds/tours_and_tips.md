# Commercial winter tours, photo workshops and practical winter-shooting tips for Mormon Row (Moulton barns), late Dec / early Jan

Research note, 27 Nov 2026. Most operator websites could not be opened from this session: jacksonholewildlifesafaris.com, tetonscience.org, outdoorproject.com and gregdisch.com do not resolve here because they are outside the allowed network list. Findings from those sites come from search-engine summaries of their pages, not from reading the pages. nps.gov pages and the NPS workshop operating plan PDF were read in full. Times are Mountain Standard Time (MST). Eastern (EST) is MST + 2 h.

## Q1. Which commercial operators bring people to Mormon Row in late Dec / early Jan, with what group sizes and at what times?

### Takeaway
Several winter wildlife-tour companies list Mormon Row as a stop, but in winter they drive the plowed roads, so their clients see the barns from the end of the plowed section (about 3/4 to 1 mile away) or from Hwy 89. I found no tour or workshop that skis or snowshoes in to the barns at sunrise. The photo workshops that do plan a Moulton-barn sunrise run in mid-November to early December, not over the Christmas–New Year week. The likely commercial presence at the barns themselves at sunrise in late December is near zero. Vans of up to 8 people may pass on Hwy 89 or stop at the Antelope Flats plow turnout mid-morning.

**What the park allows workshops to do (NPS operating plan)**
- Grand Teton's photography/painting workshop operating plan sets these limits:
  - Maximum group size is 10 participants plus a group leader, in caravans of up to 5 vehicles.
  - Workshops are allowed "in front country areas open to the public and on hiking trails within ½ mile of the road".
  - "Walks of ½ mile or less are authorized on official trails originating from official trailheads and designated turnouts and parking lots."
  - Workshops "must not conflict with the public's normal use of park areas. Visitors may not be asked to move."
  — [NPS GRTE 2024 Operating Plan, Photography & Painting Workshops (PDF)](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/upload/2024-Operating-Plan-Photography-Painting-Workshops.pdf)
- The same plan bans attaching equipment to any historic object or building, and bans lighting the landscape for night photography (light painting). — [same PDF](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/upload/2024-Operating-Plan-Photography-Painting-Workshops.pdf)
- For comparison, Yellowstone allows photography workshops of 15 people including guides. — [NPS YELL CUA for painting and photography workshops](https://www.nps.gov/yell/getinvolved/cua-for-guided-painting-and-photography-workshops.htm)

### Inferences
- The barns are about 3/4 to 1 mile past the winter plow turnout (see Q2 and the companion file `firsthand_accounts.md`). The NPS caps workshop walks at ½ mile from a road or turnout. So a commercial photo workshop is very likely not allowed to walk its clients out to the barns in winter. That is the most likely reason winter workshops shoot the barns only while the road is open, in mid-November to early December. This is a reading of the operating plan, not something any source states. Check with the NPS Business Resources Branch (307-739-3417) if it matters.
- Wildlife vans (max 6–12, mostly 8) are the main commercial traffic in late December. They run sunrise tours (7:30 a.m. start from Jackson, so about 7:50–8:15 at Antelope Flats) and half-day tours. At most they would pull into the plow turnout or stop on Hwy 89 for a long-lens view of the barns. They would not put people or footprints in the foreground at the barns.
- Expected commercial head-count at the T.A. Moulton barn at sunrise in late December: roughly zero. At the plow turnout: perhaps one or two vans an hour mid-morning (an estimate; no operator publishes daily counts).

### Gaps
- None of the operators publish which days or times their winter routes stop near Mormon Row. Routes vary with the wildlife and with closures.
- No published guided ski or snowshoe trip to Mormon Row was found. The guided ski and snowshoe outfitters (Eco Tour Adventures, TSS, Grand Teton National Park Foundation programmes) list Teton Park Road, Taggart Lake and similar routes in the results, not Mormon Row. GTNPF was not checked directly (no result surfaced).
- Holiday-week operation (24 Dec – 1 Jan) could not be confirmed for any operator, because their pages could not be opened from here.
- The park's list of current CUA holders for photography workshops was not found.

## Q2. Practical winter-shooting advice for Mormon Row, adapted to a black-and-white 4x5 film photographer

### Takeaway
In winter the barns are reached only on foot, skis or snowshoes. Park at the plowed turnout on Antelope Flats Road near Hwy 89, then go about 3/4 to 1 mile east on the unplowed road to the Mormon Row junction. The T.A. Moulton barn is a little north of the junction; the John Moulton barns are south. The longer southern approach comes from the Gros Ventre / Kelly side. Late-December sunrise is about 7:57 AM MST, with civil dawn at 7:24 and alpenglow on the peaks just before sunrise. Plan to leave the car about 6:45 AM in the dark. Expect sub-zero cold and wind on the open flat. The main film risks are brittle film and static when pulling dark slides, slow shutters, condensation and frost on the ground glass from breath, and condensation when going back indoors.

### Cited Findings
**Access, parking, route**
- NPS: Antelope Flats Road is closed in winter. The listed season for the Mormon Row page is spring, summer and fall. The north side is reached from a gravel lot via an old dirt road "level but has ruts and holes". Suggested visit length is 1–2 hours. Pets are not allowed. — [NPS: Mormon Row things to do](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/mormonrow.htm)
- NPS winter page: Antelope Flats Road is a named place to cross-country ski and snowshoe in winter. Teton Park Road is non-motorised 1 Nov – 30 Apr (Antelope Flats closure dates are not given). — [NPS GRTE: Winter in Grand Teton](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/winter.htm)
- In winter Antelope Flats Road is "only plowed for the first mile to a parking turnout", from which you ski or snowshoe to the barns. The road to the homesteads is described as about 1.5 miles (2.4 km). An outdoor guide gives closure as 1 Nov – 30 Apr, while another source says the road is open mid-March to mid-November depending on snow. The sources conflict. — [JacksonHoleWY.net: Mormon Row](https://www.jacksonholewy.net/park_highlights/mormon_row.php); [Outdoor Project: Mormon Row](https://outdoorproject.com/united-states/wyoming/mormon-row?page=1) (search summaries)
- Moon guide: Antelope Flats Road offers winter touring, "although its wide-open plain can kick with wind". — [Moon: Grand Teton winter activities](https://www.moon.com/?p=578666) (search summary)
- One photo guide says that when Antelope Flats Road is closed you can come in from Gros Ventre Road off Hwy 89 (the southern route). It also notes the south barn is easy to reach "except in the winter". — [Where Are Those Morgans: Mormon Row sunrise/sunset guide](https://wherearethosemorgans.com/mormon-row-grand-teton-sunrise-sunset/) (search summary)
- Wildlife winter closures nearby: the Snake River bottom from Moose to Moran Junction, and areas around Kelly Hill, 15 Dec – 1 Apr. Keep at least 100 yards from bears and wolves and 25 yards from other wildlife. "Winter trails are neither marked nor flagged." Snowshoers should keep off ski tracks and travel parallel to them. — [NPS GRTE: Cross-country skiing & snowshoeing](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/skiing-and-snowshoeing.htm)
- Safety from the NPS: hypothermia and frostbite "can set in quickly", and travelling alone is especially risky, so leave an itinerary with someone. For emergencies call 911; for park information call 307-739-3399. — [NPS GRTE: Winter in Grand Teton](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/winter.htm); [NPS GRTE: skiing & snowshoeing](https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/skiing-and-snowshoeing.htm)

**Light timing (computed, not from a web source)**
- Calculated at the Mormon Row barns (43.665 N, 110.666 W, NOAA sun formulas, flat horizon), in MST:

  | Date | Nautical dawn | Civil dawn | Sunrise | Sunrise direction |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 21 Dec | 6:44 | 7:21 | 7:54 | 123° (ESE) |
  | 29 Dec – 4 Jan | 6:47–6:48 | 7:24 | 7:56–7:57 | 121–122° |

  In Eastern time, sunrise is 9:57 AM EST. The Tetons lie due west of the barns, so the sun rises behind the photographer and lights the peaks front-on. First light touches the summits a few minutes before flat-horizon sunrise and works down the face. The sun stays low all day (about 23° at noon), so side light on the barn's east face lasts well into the morning.

**Crowds, etiquette and footprints**
- Guides describe 20–50 photographers at sunrise in peak season, and 5–20 at the south (John Moulton) barns. The location listing rates the winter crowd as "just a few people". — [Locationscout: Mormon Row barns](https://www.locationscout.net/usa/245-mormon-row-barns); [Locationscout: Mormon settlement barn](https://www.locationscout.net/usa/245-mormon-settlement-barn) (search summaries)
- Watch your own long shadow at sunrise. Frame with trees, ditches, fences, meadows and mountains. Be courteous: "you're not the only person who has made the effort to be at Mormon Row for sunrise". — [Where Are Those Morgans](https://wherearethosemorgans.com/mormon-row-grand-teton-sunrise-sunset/)
- General winter advice: "act fast when snow is fresh", because fresh snow gets tracked quickly. — [search summary of general winter photography guides, e.g. Adorama snow photography tips](https://www.adorama.com/alc/tips-snow-photography)
- No source describing footprint management at Mormon Row specifically was found (companion file `firsthand_accounts.md` reached the same result).

**Cold and large-format film**
- LF photographers in the cold report:
  - Film becomes brittle, and breakage is the real risk; the emulsion itself works fine.
  - Static discharge marks are possible in dry cold air, so move dark slides and film slowly.
  - Breath on the ground glass condenses and can freeze instantly, making focusing hard.
  - The biggest problem is condensation when bringing cold gear indoors, so let it warm slowly in a sealed bag.
  - Shutters run sluggishly below about -20 °C (-4 °F). Have old shutters cleaned and relubricated for the cold.
  - Keep the meter (battery) warm between readings.
  - Wear thin insulated gloves under tethered mittens.
  Experiences vary: some report no issues down to -40. — [Photrio: Large format at low temperature](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/large-format-at-low-temperature.4839/); [LargeFormatPhotography.info: Dealing with the cold](https://www.largeformatphotography.info/forum/archive/index.php/t-44077.html); [Photrio: Shooting in sub-zero conditions](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/shooting-in-sub-zero-conditions.35834); [Photrio: How cold can film get before cracking?](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/how-cold-can-film-get-before-cracking.26719/?amp=1)

### Inferences (practical plan for a B&W 4x5 shooter; reasoning from the findings above)
- **Approach:**
  - Park at the Antelope Flats plow turnout by about 6:30 AM MST (8:30 EST).
  - Leave by 6:45 with a headlamp, on skis or snowshoes.
  - About 3/4–1 mile on a flat, unplowed road takes 20–35 min with a pack, depending on snow and any trail broken by earlier visitors.
  - That puts you at the T.A. Moulton barn by about 7:15, before civil dawn (7:24). There is then time to pick a spot, set up and focus on the peaks while the sky brightens.
  - Snowshoes are easier with a heavy 4x5 pack and tripod and for stepping off the road into deep untracked snow for a foreground. Skis are faster on the road if a track exists.
  - The route is flat with no avalanche terrain, but it is exposed to wind.
- **Footprints:**
  - Come in along the road, then circle to your camera spot along the fence line or the road edge, so the untracked snow between camera and barn stays clean.
  - After a fresh snowfall, the first visitor of the day decides the foreground for everyone. Arriving first on a post-storm clear morning is worth the most.
  - Other photographers' tracks from earlier days will be in the common spots (directly east of the T.A. barn). Off-axis angles, such as the north-east angle Phil already has as a shot, are less likely to be trampled.
- **Light:**
  - Alpenglow on the Grand comes about 7:45–7:57 AM MST; sun on the barn comes a few minutes after sunrise.
  - For B&W, the front-lit peaks against a dark pre-dawn western sky give strong tonal separation. A red or orange filter darkens the blue sky further after sunrise. With snow, meter carefully: place the snow at Zone VII–VIII.
  - Long exposures and reciprocity failure are likely in the blue hour.
- **Cold (about 5 °F / -15 °C, colder with wind):**
  - Pre-load holders indoors.
  - Pull and replace dark slides slowly (static, brittle film).
  - Keep the meter and spare batteries inside your jacket.
  - Don't breathe on the ground glass; use a focusing cloth and a loupe and hold your breath.
  - Tension a cold bellows gently.
  - Bag the camera and holders before going back to the car or room.
  - Wear liner gloves under mittens.
  - Carry a thermos and a foam pad to stand on.
- **Other photographers:** In late December you can expect anything from no one to a handful of independent photographers who walked in. Arrive earliest, and don't set up in front of anyone. The NPS rule "visitors may not be asked to move" binds workshops, and it is good manners for everyone.
- **Check before the day:** look at the NPS conditions page. The project's plan checks in November 2026 flagged building work at both barns, and fencing or crews would change the frame.

### Gaps
- No official NPS source gives the exact winter plow end point or turnout location for Antelope Flats Road. The distance from the turnout varies by source: 3/4 mile, about 1 mile, or 1.5 miles from Hwy 89.
- The Gros Ventre / Kelly southern approach (length, plowing, whether it crosses the Kelly Hill wildlife closure, 15 Dec – 1 Apr) could not be confirmed from a primary source.
- No source measured typical December wind or temperature at Antelope Flats itself. "About 5 °F" is the brief's figure.
- How often valley inversion fog sits on Antelope Flats at sunrise in late December was not researched here. Per the project notes, KJAC records fog in about 8–9% of 08–09 MST hours in Dec/Jan.
