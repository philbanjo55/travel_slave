Round 1 · done this morning

One day at a time: the forecast added nothing

117 past Grand Teton days, each planned the evening before and scored against the satellite. Points are your tiers (GOD 10, S 5, A 2, B and C 1) times how good the light was.

The planner did well in absolute terms, but a plan from sun angles alone did just as well. Your own day scores low because skiing, travel and meals count as zero here.

Why: the test was too kind to grey days

A grey day still scored about two-thirds of a sunny one, because the old forecasts don't say whether the Grand was hidden in cloud. When every day is worth about the same, reading the forecast can't win much.

Round 2 · running now

Two fixes so the test asks the real question

Hidden peaks count as nearly nothing

When the airport reported cloud below the summits, the shot is worth a tenth: under 7,000 ft above Jackson (about the Grand's top), under 2,000 ft at Vágar, or fog. Each forecast's chance of hidden peaks is learned from the other years only, so no year grades its own homework.

Forecast says clear: peaks still hidden 10–20% of daylight hours. Forecast says overcast: hidden about 70%.
